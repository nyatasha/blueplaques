Bloomsbury & Fitzrovia, WC1

Bloomsbury Writers & Thinkers

Dickens, Darwin and the Bloomsbury Group in one square mile.

8 stops about 3.0 km about 38 min walking

Bloomsbury has the densest concentration of blue plaques in London, and they are almost all people who wrote, argued or discovered something. This walk starts at the Doughty Street house where Charles Dickens wrote Oliver Twist, crosses to the Gower Street terrace where the Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood was founded, runs through the Georgian squares the Bloomsbury Group made their own, and ends where Virginia Woolf was living when those Thursday evenings began. Charles Darwin's site, where he wrote up the Beagle voyage, is on the way.

Start at Russell Square or Chancery Lane; finish at Warren Street.

The route

  1. Charles Dickens

    1812–1870 · 48 Doughty Street, Camden, WC1

    Dickens lived here from 1837 to 1839 and wrote Oliver Twist and Nicholas Nickleby in the house. It is the only one of his London homes still standing, and is now the Charles Dickens Museum.

  2. about 1.3 km from the last stop

    Millicent Garrett Fawcett

    1847–1929 · 2 Gower Street, Camden, WC1

    The leader of the constitutional suffrage campaign lived and died in this house — she led the National Union of Women's Suffrage Societies for twenty years before the vote was won.

  3. about 45 m from the last stop

    The Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood

    7 Gower Street, Camden, WC1

    Not a person but a moment: seven young artists founded the Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood in this house in 1848, two doors from Fawcett's.

  4. about 466 m from the last stop

    Christina Rossetti

    30 Torrington Square, Camden, WC1

    The poet of Goblin Market lived and died on Torrington Square — her brother Dante Gabriel appears later on the Chelsea walk.

  5. about 161 m from the last stop

    Lytton Strachey

    1880–1932 · 51 Gordon Square, Camden, WC1

    Strachey's Eminent Victorians made him famous and made Victorian biography impossible to write straight again. Gordon Square was the Bloomsbury Group's centre of gravity.

  6. about 45 m from the last stop

    John Maynard Keynes

    1883–1946 · 46 Gordon Square, Bloomsbury, Camden, WC1

    Keynes lived at number 46 for thirty years, from the Versailles conference to the founding of the IMF, four doors from Strachey.

  7. about 330 m from the last stop

    Charles Darwin

    1809–1882 · Biological Sciences Building, University College, (site of 110) Gower Street, Camden, WC1

    Darwin lived on this site from 1838 to 1842, the years he worked up the Beagle notebooks and first sketched natural selection. The house is gone; UCL's Biological Sciences building stands here.

  8. about 655 m from the last stop

    Virginia Woolf

    1882–1941 · 29 Fitzroy Square, Camden, W1

    Woolf lived here as Virginia Stephen from 1907 to 1911, before her marriage and before her first novel — the Thursday evenings in this house are where the Bloomsbury Group began.
