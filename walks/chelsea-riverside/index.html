Chelsea, SW3 & SW10

Chelsea Riverside

Oscar Wilde, Bob Marley and a mile of the Thames.

8 stops about 1.7 km about 21 min walking

Cheyne Walk and the streets running off it hold one of the finest runs of plaques in London, and they sit almost in a straight line along the river. This walk covers a mile between Tite Street, where Oscar Wilde wrote his plays and was arrested, and the far end of Cheyne Walk. On the way it passes the house Bob Marley rented while recording Exodus, the room where penicillin's discoverer lived, and the stretch of riverfront that Whistler painted.

Start near Sloane Square; finish near Chelsea Harbour or walk back along the Embankment.

The route

  1. Oscar Wilde

    1854–1900 · 34 Tite Street, Kensington and Chelsea, SW3

    Wilde lived at 34 Tite Street from 1884 until 1895, wrote The Importance of Being Earnest here, and was arrested here.

  2. about 378 m from the last stop

    George Eliot

    1819–1880 · 4 Cheyne Walk, Kensington and Chelsea, SW3

    Mary Ann Evans moved into 4 Cheyne Walk in December 1880 and died three weeks later. The house looks straight out at the river.

  3. about 152 m from the last stop

    Algernon Charles Swinburne

    1828–1882 · 16 Cheyne Walk, Kensington and Chelsea, SW3

    One plaque, two poets: Dante Gabriel Rossetti and Algernon Charles Swinburne shared this house — Rossetti kept a menagerie in the garden, wombat included.

  4. about 220 m from the last stop

    Bob Marley

    1945–1981 · 42 Oakley Street, Chelsea

    Marley rented this house in 1977 while the Wailers were in London recording Exodus and Kaya. One of the newer plaques on the walk, put up in 2019.

  5. about 478 m from the last stop

    Alexander Fleming

    1881–1955 · 20a Danvers Street

    Fleming had already found penicillin at St Mary's in 1928; he lived here in Danvers Street for the rest of his life.

  6. about 184 m from the last stop

    Elizabeth Gaskell

    1810–1865 · 93 Cheyne Walk, Kensington and Chelsea, SW10

    Gaskell was born at 93 Cheyne Walk in 1810. Her plaque is one of the oldest on this walk, put up in 1913.

  7. about 26 m from the last stop

    James Abbott McNeill Whistler

    1834–1903 · 96 Cheyne Walk, Kensington and Chelsea, SW10

    Whistler lived next door but one to Gaskell's birthplace, and the river outside is the one in his Nocturnes.

  8. about 262 m from the last stop

    Sylvia Pankhurst

    1882–1960 · 120 Cheyne Walk, Kensington and Chelsea, SW10

    The most radical of the Pankhursts lived at the far end of Cheyne Walk — East End organiser, anti-fascist, and eventually a citizen of Ethiopia.
